    Two Weeks in York

    Last spring, Masha, a fifteen-year-old student from Kazan, took part in a school exchange programme and spent two weeks in York, a historic city in the north of England. She stayed with the Hendersons, a family with two children. Their daughter Lucy was the same age as Masha, and the two girls became friends on the very first day.

    Masha was a little nervous before the trip. She had studied English for eight years, but she had never spoken to native speakers outside the classroom. "In the first few days I understood only half of what people said," she remembers. "They spoke very fast and used a lot of words I didn't know." But step by step it became easier, and by the end of the first week she could chat with Lucy's friends without any problems.

    Every morning Masha went to school with Lucy. The lessons were different from those at home. There were fewer students in each class, and they often worked in groups. Masha's favourite subject was drama, which is not taught at her school in Kazan.

    At the weekends the Hendersons showed Masha around. They visited York Minster, one of the largest cathedrals in Northern Europe, and walked along the old city walls. Masha also tried traditional English food. She loved fish and chips but did not like the famous English breakfast with beans.

    Now Masha and Lucy write to each other almost every day. In summer Lucy is going to come to Kazan, and Masha is already planning what to show her.
